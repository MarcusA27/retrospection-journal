# Every night you rebuilt the part that worked and avoided the part that scared you

This was the week you started Stash, and the week you found seven different ways not to finish it. The shape repeats so cleanly it's almost a rule: each session, the moment the work got comfortable, you went looking for harder work.

It started with the stack you'd already chosen — "ok node. boring is good" (Sep 8) — relitigated by midnight into "what about a local-first thing with sqlite." Then a night building auth for an app with one user (Sep 10), then ripping that auth out entirely — "honestly im overcomplicating this" (Sep 11) — in a rewrite that, notably, kept the save button and the parser. The parts that always worked, you protected. The parts that scared you, you rebuilt or deleted.

The clearest tell was Saturday: a full afternoon on a landing page — "whats a good headline for a bookmarking app" (Sep 13) — for an app that couldn't tag a bookmark yet. You can describe Stash beautifully; you keep not building the thing it describes. The one time the real question surfaced — "who is this even for" (Sep 14) — you closed it with "anyway, deploy next week."

What the rewrites have in common isn't progress. It's that they're all somewhere safe to stand. Building is the part you control completely. Nothing you did this week could be judged by anyone but you — because no one but you has seen it.
